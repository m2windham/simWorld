## Storyteller probe - the threat curve over 60 in-game days at founding scale, populated world (`--solo false`)


> One arm: **watched and played**. The founding settlement is opened, so its citizens run at Full tier and a threat arrives on a real map. **World: populated** - rival civilizations are generated at time zero, as a player's world has them. Table 0 lists who they are and where they stand with the player. Founding band 20 (spec 5b.3 allows 20-40), seed 99, storyteller as `Game.NewGame` defaults it, **threats off** (`--threats false`): Medium in every knob except that big and intro threats may not fire. This is the baseline arm - what these people do to themselves with nobody attacking them. **This suite changes no tuning constant and is not a target** - see its class doc, and `CLAUDE.md` on what happens to a game tuned against its own instruments.

> `wealth` is `IIncidentTarget.PlayerWealthForStoryteller` - what the storyteller reads, not a figure computed here. `pop` is the roster the curve itself sums over (`PlayerPawnsForStoryteller`), which is the attended slice, not the head count; table 3 carries `GodRollup.TotalPopulation` beside it. `downed` is how many of that roster were down at the day's sample - a day-boundary reading, so a fight that began and ended inside one day can pass under it. `clamped?` is `min` or `max` when `GenMath.Clamp` actually bound the result at 35 or 10,000 - **a run sitting on a clamp is a run in which the whole curve above it is doing nothing**. reconstruction: 61/61 days where these columns multiply back to `DefaultThreatPointsNow`'s own answer.

> `points at fire` is the `IncidentParms.points` the worker was actually handed, which is **not** table 1's daily sample and is not meant to be. `StorytellerUtility.DefaultParmsNow` gives points only to the two threat categories, so every other category reads 0.0 and that means "this incident does not spend threat points", not "the storyteller had none". A threat incident marked `pointsScaleable` is additionally jittered by its comp's `randomPointsFactorRange` (`StorytellerComp_RandomMain`), so it lands beside the curve rather than on it.

> `fired?` is the worker's own return value, read off the chronicle tail by object identity - see the class doc. **`nothing (stub worker)` is not a formatting choice**: 0 of 8 firings ran a worker whose effect method is a bare `return true`, detected from its IL rather than from a list of names. 0 firings were refused by their worker (`fired? = no`), and 3 fired a real worker that moved nothing this instrument can see (`no observable effect`), which is a weaker claim than "it did nothing" - the class doc lists exactly what the column can and cannot see, and pawns downed is not on it.

> **Orphaned attribution:** a source-keyed ledger grew for a defName with no open firing (killed 0, razed 87, denied 0.0). That harm happened and this table does not show which firing owns it. It is a defect in this instrument, not in the game.

### 4. is it deterministic

| series | days compared | identical |
| --- | --- | --- |
| threat points (the `points` column) | 8 | yes |
| curve inputs (wealth, per-colonist, the four factors) | 8 | yes |
| world beside it (pop, mood, food, deaths, moments) | 8 | yes |
| firing log | 8 | yes |

> A **prefix** comparison over 8 days: two runs of one seed either agree from the first sample or they do not. The run is split into four series because they can fail apart, and which one failed is the whole of the information. The threat points are a pure function of the roster, the stores and the clock; the curve inputs are where those are read; the world beside them is what the settlement actually did; and the firing log is where the seeded draws land. **Points can replay exactly while the world under them does not** - below the wealth curve's 14,000 floor the points do not read stores at all, so a settlement that hauled one fewer meal moves the world rows and cannot move the points row.
